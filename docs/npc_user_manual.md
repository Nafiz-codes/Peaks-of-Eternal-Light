# Lunar Expedition NPC User Manual

You are the expedition's player-controlled astronaut. Your four existing crew
members now carry out fieldwork as well as their original station duties.
Their familiar names and armband colors have stayed the same.

## Meet your crew

| Astronaut | Armband | Added job | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Water specialist** | Light blue | Geologist | Walks with you to a sample marker, examines and photographs a rock, collects it with tongs, labels it, and carries it to storage. |
| **Energy specialist** | Dark blue | Instrument specialist | Travels with the equipment specialist, positions and verifies a seismometer, photographs the installation, and reports its status. |
| **Food specialist** | Light green | Equipment specialist / engineer | Inspects the rover, checks its condition and power, repairs it when assigned and supplied, and assists instrument deployment. |
| **Crew lead** | Purple | Navigator / coordinator | Reviews marked routes, reports crew status and return requirements, and keeps an activity log. Can accompany you locally. |

The original water, power, food and crew reports are still available in each
astronaut's conversation window. Scroll down to find them.

## Quick start

1. Start a mission using the mission controls or a crew conversation.
2. Approach an astronaut and press **E** to open their **Crew radio** window.
3. Read their specialty, current state and short explanation at the top.
4. If they are **Available**, choose an assignment. If they are already working,
   use **Talk / status**, **Assist current step**, or the interruption controls.
5. Close the conversation with **End conversation** or **Esc** to walk with them.
6. Reopen the conversation to help, change orders or review results.

About **20 seconds** after mission startup, each available astronaut attempts an
initial job. They may already be preparing, waiting for you, or helping a colleague
when you first speak to them. They do not endlessly repeat completed jobs: assign
further work yourself.

## Conversation controls

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Talk / status** | Opens the detailed work report. |
| **Status / records and activity log** | Expands or collapses recorded results and detailed status. |
| **Assign…** | Starts one of that specialist's tasks. Available while they are free. |
| **Assist current step** | Helps prepare tools, carry equipment or perform the current work step. Stand within roughly **3 m** of the astronaut. |
| **Pause** | Stops work at its current checkpoint. Carried items stay with the astronaut. |
| **Resume / retry** | Attempts to continue after a pause, missing helper, missing supply or cleared obstruction. |
| **Follow** | Asks an available astronaut to accompany you within the established local area. Approach them before requesting it. |
| **Return to base** | Ends the current outing and sends the astronaut back along a checked path. Collected samples remain with their carrier until deposited. |
| **Regroup crew** | Orders crew with active tasks to return to their base positions. It does not teleport them. |

Only one science field team uses the shared excursion resources at a time.
If another team has the tools, finish or return that team before starting yours.

## Water specialist: collecting samples

**A good first outing**

1. Approach the light-blue astronaut and press **E**.
2. Choose **Assign: sample A (accompany on foot)**. If the geologist already has
   this assignment and is waiting, stand nearby and choose **Resume / retry**.
3. Close the conversation and follow them to **SAMPLE A**. Stay within **6 m**.
4. They inspect the surroundings, photograph the undisturbed sample, collect it
   with tongs, and label its location. You can approach and **Assist current step**.
5. Let them carry the sample back to **FIELD SUPPLIES / SAMPLE STORAGE**.
6. Open **Talk / status** to review the sample's location, observations and whether
   it is still **carried** or already at **base**.

The rock disappears from its collection site when collected. A stored container
becomes visible at the locker after delivery. A sample cannot be collected twice.

**Optional detour:** when the geologist is available, choose **Approve optional
sample B detour**, or **Decline detour / stay at base**. To change an active outing,
return them to base first.

**If they need a container:** collect one at the supplies locker, bring it to the
geologist, choose **Deliver container**, then **Resume / retry**.

## Energy specialist: installing an instrument

1. Approach the dark-blue astronaut and choose deployment at **instrument A** or
   **instrument B**.
2. The light-green equipment specialist joins as a buddy. If that astronaut is
   busy, finish or return their task before retrying deployment.
3. The pair travel to the marked site. You can help with preparation or carrying
   by staying nearby and choosing **Assist current step**.
4. The specialist unpacks the seismometer, positions and levels it, charges and
   verifies it, then photographs the installation.
5. Review its status through **Talk / status**. Successful work makes the visible
   instrument **operational**.

The seismometer reports its self-test, operating time and remaining battery time.
It records locally; it does not need a cable to the base. There are currently no
simulated seismic waveforms or moonquake measurements.

**If verification needs power:** ask the equipment specialist to **Allocate limited
power to science**, then resume the instrument task. The mission must also have
enough stored energy.

**If you interrupt installation:** return the team, then reassign deployment.
They resume the existing installation rather than creating another instrument.
After battery depletion, use the deployment assignment again to service the same
instrument. The default battery lasts about **40 minutes of active game time**.

## Food specialist: inspections and rover repairs

1. Park the rover and leave remote driving mode.
2. Approach the light-green astronaut and choose **Assign rover readiness / dust
   inspection**.
3. They travel to the rover, inspect its energy and condition, visually check
   exposed equipment, and record readiness. An inspection alone does not repair it.
4. If the rover is damaged, choose **Allocate limited power to maintenance**, then
   assign rover maintenance when the specialist is available.
5. A repair uses **one replacement kit and one mission material**, restores up to
   **40 health**, and is followed by verification.

You can help with the current step or deliver a carried replacement kit. When the
engineer is assisting instrument deployment, complete or end that outing before
requesting maintenance.

The player's direct **E** rover repair is still available while on foot beside the
rover. It also consumes a kit and material, and cannot use a worksite reserved by
the engineer. Outside the local base area, carry a kit with you.

**Supplies are finite.** If kits or mission materials run out, returning to base does
not create more. You may need to use a damaged rover, travel on foot, or change plans.

## Crew lead: routes, status and coordination

Approach the purple astronaut and choose:

- **Assign local route / return-reserve review** to have them complete a route and
  resource assessment from their base position.
- **Assess destination: …** to check a specific marked sample or instrument site.
- **Talk / status** to review crew reports, route distance, estimated round-trip
  oxygen use and recent mission activities.
- **Follow** for local accompaniment, or **Regroup crew** to bring active teams back.

Routes are limited to the established landing area, roughly **14 m** from its
center. Unknown terrain is not assessed. During a communication blackout, or beyond
the local radio range, crew positions are reported as **unconfirmed**.

## Supplies: picking up and delivering items

Find the marked **FIELD SUPPLIES / SAMPLE STORAGE** locker near the crew stations.
It starts with **four sample containers and three replacement kits**.

1. Stand near the locker and open a crew conversation.
2. Choose **Pick up container at locker** or **Pick up repair kit at locker**.
3. Walk to the relevant astronaut: container to the geologist, kit to the engineer.
4. Choose **Deliver…**, then **Resume / retry** if they were waiting for it.

You can carry one of each item. NPCs normally prepare their own supplies from this
same stock. A used sample container stays with its sample; a used repair kit is
consumed. Unused supplies carried by an NPC return to stock when they return to base.

## What their state means

| State | What is happening / what you should do |
| --- | --- |
| **Available** | Ready for an assignment. |
| **Preparing** | Checking and packing the equipment. You can help. |
| **Traveling** | Walking to the worksite. Accompany the geologist. |
| **Working** | Performing the current inspection, photograph, collection, setup or maintenance step. |
| **Recording results** | Saving observations and preparing to return. |
| **Returning** | Carrying equipment or samples back. Wait for arrival before expecting storage to change. |
| **Waiting for assistance** | Needs a companion, container, part or setup helper. Read the explanation, provide it, then resume. |
| **Blocked** | A route, tool, equipment or resource requirement prevents progress. Resolve the stated blocker and retry. |
| **Paused** | You stopped this job. Choose resume or return. |
| **Responding to a hazard** | A mission decision, low oxygen reserve or solar-particle event stopped optional work. Resolve the issue or order a checked return. |

## When something stops working

| Problem | What to try |
| --- | --- |
| “Companion out of range” | Walk back within 6 m of the geologist and resume. |
| Shared tool or worksite reserved | Finish, pause or return the astronaut using it. Then retry. |
| Another crew member occupies the path | Move the other astronaut with Follow/Return, or regroup. Then resume the blocked task. |
| Route blocked | Choose **Assess alternate approach**, then **Approve assessed alternative** if one is found. Otherwise clear the obstruction or return. |
| No safe return path | Choose a different site or clear the route. NPCs do not teleport through obstacles. |
| Repair needs a kit or materials | Deliver an available kit and check mission materials. Do not expect unlimited repairs. |
| Instrument waits for verification | Let the engineer reach the site, or stand beside the specialist and assist. Check science power allocation. |
| Assignment buttons are missing | The astronaut is busy, or a mission decision must be resolved. Read their state; return them if you want a different assignment. |
| Sample not yet in storage | It may still be carried. Allow the geologist to deliver and label it. |

## Saving and game assumptions

Mission saves retain crew positions, task progress, carried samples, stored results,
equipment state and supplies. The game pause freezes fieldwork.

The distances, short work steps and resource costs above are the current gameplay
defaults. Photography records a visible action and documentation metadata rather
than saving a screenshot. Tool motions are simplified, and dust inspections do not
simulate a separate dust-damage system.

For implementation details and scientific references, see
[Crew fieldwork notes](crew_fieldwork.md).
